Making learning tangible

Bachelor thesis project

Background

The Challenge

In special education classrooms, building activities with construction bricks are often used to playfully develop spatial thinking, problem-solving and planning skills.

However, traditional bricks cannot provide feedback. Mistakes may go unnoticed until much later, making corrections frustrating and often requiring help from a teacher.

Designed for Different Needs

proBricks was developed specifically for children with special educational needs (particularly in the areas of learning, cognitive development and autism).

Since learners have very different abilities and support needs, the goal was to create an accessible, adaptable learning experience that encourages independent exploration.

The Concept

proBricks combines physical building with digital learning. The smart bricks automatically detect how they are connected and oriented, creating a real-time digital representation of the construction.

By comparing the physical structure with a digital building challenge, the system provides immediate feedback, helping children identify mistakes and find solutions more independently.

Interaction

How to Play

Learners select a 3D target structure in the learning application and recreate it using physical bricks.

As the bricks are connected, the system automatically detects their arrangement and creates a digital twin of the construction. By continuously comparing it with the target, learners receive feedback throughout the building process.

Feedback That Guides

Feedback is provided both digitally and directly on the physical bricks.

Integrated LEDs indicate correct or incorrect placements, while the learning application visualizes the construction, highlights mistakes and tracks progress. Transparent ghost bricks can even suggest where to build next.

One System, Different Needs

From simple structures to complex 3D challenges, task difficulty can be adapted to different abilities and learning needs.

The digital learning application also opens up possibilities for different levels of guidance, multimodal feedback and new game modes — all using the same physical bricks.

Technology

Anatomy of a Brick

Each smart brick contains a custom-designed PCB with a microcontroller and integrated feedback LEDs, enclosed in a modular, 3D-printed housing.

The bricks combine magnetic attraction with mechanical guidance, making connections easy to establish while ensuring precise alignment. The modular housing also allows individual components to be assembled, replaced and modified.

One Interface, Multiple Functions

Connection

A single brick-to-brick interface combines five essential functions:

Mechanical Connection

Magnets and interlocking geometry guide the bricks into position and provide a stable, easy-to-use connection.

Rotation Detection

Four contacts on the docking face interact with a single contact on the receiving face, identifying the brick's relative rotation in 90° increments.

Connection Detection

Each brick identifies which of its receiving faces are connected to neighboring bricks.

Data Communication

The same electrical contacts used for orientation detection enable bidirectional data communication between connected bricks.

Power Supply

Concentrically arranged power contacts distribute electricity from the baseplate throughout the entire construction, eliminating the need for individual batteries.

Usertests

Building with the Students

Insights

The first prototype was tested at a special education school. Seeing how excited the children were to try proBricks was one of the highlights of the project. The children enthusiastically explored different building challenges and experimented independently.

What's Next?

The flexible learning platform opens up possibilities for new game modes, multimodal feedback and collaborative challenges. Connecting physical constructions with digital worlds could create entirely new experiences.

Beyond special education, proBricks could also find applications in mainstream schools, therapy and rehabilitation, or learning activities for older adults.
